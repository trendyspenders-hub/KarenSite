Metro Vancouver

Safety Programs Built for Vancouver's Economy

Vancouver is the economic engine of British Columbia, and it is also one of the most demanding places in the province to run a compliant workplace. The Port of Vancouver alone moves roughly $300 billion in trade a year and supports more than 130,000 jobs across the region, while construction employment in Metro Vancouver has more than doubled over the past two decades. Add Canada's third-largest film production centre, a tech sector employing close to 200,000 people, and a hospitality industry serving millions of visitors, and you get a city where nearly every employer has real, documentable safety obligations under the Workers Compensation Act.

What makes Vancouver different is density. Nowhere else in B.C. do so many employers share the same footprint: tower cranes swinging over public sidewalks, half a dozen subcontractors stacked onto a single downtown site, film crews transforming a city block overnight, delivery vehicles cycling through the same constrained loading docks. Dense sites mean overlapping hazards — and WorkSafeBC is explicit that on multiple-employer workplaces, coordination duties are not optional. A plan written for a quiet single-tenant shop will not survive contact with a Vancouver jobsite.

That is where we come in. Alcantara Safety Solutions works with Vancouver employers of every size — from five-person trades subcontractors bidding their first prime contract to established property managers responsible for entire downtown portfolios. Every plan is written from scratch against the WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation, your industry's specific parts of the Regulation, and the realities of working in this city: tight sites, high public exposure, sophisticated general contractors, and inspectors who have seen everything.

Local Industries

Who We Serve in Vancouver

Construction & Development

Vancouver's skyline keeps growing, and so do the compliance expectations on every site. Downtown tower projects, Broadway corridor densification, and infill builds across the East Side all share the same pressures: prime contractor coordination under the Workers Compensation Act, fall protection on complex geometries, silica exposure from concrete work, crane and lift planning over occupied streets, and orientations for crews that change weekly. We build site-specific safety plans and subcontractor packages that general contractors and developers actually accept — because they address the coordination, documentation, and inspection-readiness questions primes ask first.

Film & Production Support

Hollywood North is not a nickname — it is an economy. Productions filming across Vancouver depend on construction crews, electricians, riggers, drivers, caterers, and labourers, many of them moving between short engagements with different employers every month. If you supply trades or services to productions, your safety documentation needs to keep pace with that churn: rapid worker orientations, clear supervision structures, traffic and pedestrian control around locations, and hazard assessments for temporary builds. We help production-support contractors stay pre-qualified and inspection-ready between gigs, not just at renewal time.

Port Logistics & Warehousing

From the container terminals to the distribution facilities strung along the port corridor and the False Creek Flats, Vancouver's logistics sector works around the clock — and so do its hazards. Forklift and pedestrian interaction, container loading, racking systems, dock operations, and fatigue management on extended shifts are the issues WorkSafeBC inspectors look for first in this sector. We write warehouse safety programs with traffic management plans your supervisors can enforce, equipment inspection routines your leads will actually run, and documentation that stands up when a prime or a port tenant audits your operation.

Property Management & Hospitality

Downtown hotels, restaurant groups, strata portfolios, and commercial buildings all carry employer obligations that are easy to underestimate. Maintenance crews face electrical, roof access, and confined space hazards in mechanical rooms. Housekeeping and janitorial teams handle chemicals and repetitive strain risks. Kitchens run hot, fast, and crowded. Vancouver's hospitality and property sectors also employ large numbers of young and new workers — the group statistically most likely to be hurt in their first months on the job. Our programs for this sector lean hard on orientation quality, plain-language procedures, and supervisor accountability.

Technology & Professional Offices

Vancouver's tech and professional services employers often assume the OHS Regulation does not apply to them. It does — every B.C. employer with workers has duties around bullying and harassment policies, workplace violence prevention, ergonomics, emergency procedures, and new-worker orientation. The documentation burden is lighter than a construction site's, but it is not zero, and it is exactly the kind of gap that surfaces during a WorkSafeBC claim review or a commercial lease audit. We build right-sized programs for office employers: compliant, professional, and finished in days rather than months.

WorkSafeBC Compliance

What Vancouver Employers Need to Know

Vancouver employers operate under the same province-wide OHS Regulation as everyone else in B.C. — but they feel it more acutely. The region's inspection activity is concentrated where the workers are, and metro-area prevention officers see sophisticated operations daily. The issues we see cited most often on Vancouver files are predictable: missing or generic written programs, undocumented orientations, prime contractor coordination that exists in practice but not on paper, overdue joint committee minutes, and exposure control plans for silica and asbestos that were never updated after a scope change.

Two Vancouver-specific realities deserve attention. First, the multiple-employer workplace rules: on most Vancouver construction sites, one employer must be designated as prime contractor with written coordination responsibilities — and if nobody is designated, the owner fills that role by default. Second, public exposure. Work over or beside sidewalks, bike lanes, and occupied buildings draws complaints, and complaints draw inspections. A Vancouver safety plan that ignores public protection and traffic management is a plan with a hole in it. Ours do not.
