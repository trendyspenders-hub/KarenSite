Safety Documentation for Contractor Bids

Losing a bid over safety paperwork is more common than losing one over price. Prime contractors and owners increasingly pre-qualify bidders on their safety program before they read the number. The good news: what they ask for is predictable.

What bid packages typically ask for

  • A company health and safety policy and program overview
  • Site-specific safety plans for the proposed work
  • Hazard assessments and safe work procedures for the higher-risk tasks in scope
  • Training records and certifications for key crew members
  • Incident statistics and, sometimes, WorkSafeBC clearance letters
  • Emergency response and first aid arrangements for the site

The expensive way and the cheap way

The expensive way is rebuilding this package from scratch for every bid, usually the night before it is due. The cheap way is maintaining a core program that is always current — so a bid package becomes an assembly job, not a writing job. That is the practical difference between a template binder and a program that is actually managed.

Read the pre-qualification requirements early

Some owners require specific formats, third-party audit scores, or COR certification. Those requirements change what must be built and how long it takes, so they belong in the conversation before a bid deadline exists — not after.
