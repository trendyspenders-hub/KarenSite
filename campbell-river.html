Safety Programs Built for Campbell River's Economy

Campbell River's economy rests on foundations that are literally resource-based. Forestry has been the community's industrial bedrock for generations and remains a major employer; aquaculture has made the city the centre of the global farmed salmon industry on Canada's West Coast; and the self-described Salmon Capital of the World backs a tourism sector built on fishing, wildlife, and outdoor adventure. Around those pillars, the city has been deliberately diversifying – a growing STEM and technical services community, health and wellness services for the North Island, and the transportation and logistics operations that connect the upper Island to the rest of the province.

This is an economy where workplace hazards are not abstractions. Aquaculture means crews working over cold ocean water in all weather. Forestry and wood processing mean some of the highest-severity hazard categories in the OHS Regulation. Construction keeps pace with the city's growth, and the service economy – healthcare, retail, hospitality – carries the standard obligations that smaller-city employers often underestimate. WorkSafeBC's North Island prevention presence knows these industries well, and the region's employers are inspected accordingly.

Alcantara Safety Solutions serves employers across Campbell River and the North Island. We work on-site throughout the region, writing programs from scratch against the Regulation and the specific requirements of your industry – aquaculture, forestry, construction, tourism, or the growing service sector – at a fixed fee quoted before we begin.

Local Industries

Who We Serve in Campbell River

Aquaculture & Marine Operations

Campbell River is the centre of Canada's West Coast aquaculture industry, and farm operations carry one of the most serious hazard profiles in the provincial economy: work over cold water with drowning and immersion risks, vessel and equipment operations, net pen and mooring work in exposed conditions, and remote sites where emergency response takes real time. Processing adds machine guarding, cold storage, and sanitation hazards. The sector's regulatory overlap – federal for vessels and navigation, WorkSafeBC for crew safety ashore and on site – confuses many operators. We untangle the jurisdiction question, then write complete programs for the WorkSafeBC side: over-water procedures, PFD and rescue requirements, cold water immersion protocols, and orientation documentation that survives crew turnover.

Forestry & Wood Products

Forestry built Campbell River, and the sector – logging contractors, silviculture operations, wood processing, and the trucking that connects them – still carries some of the Regulation's highest-severity categories. Falling and bucking hazards, steep ground operations, log transportation, sawmill and processing equipment, and the prime contractor coordination that governs multi-phase forestry operations all demand documented, tested programs. The industry's current transition makes systematic safety documentation more important, not less: operations running leaner crews cannot absorb the cost of preventable incidents. We write forestry programs that meet the sector's standards – and the pre-qualification requirements of the licensees and mills contractors work for.

Construction & Trades

Campbell River's steady growth keeps its builders busy – residential development, commercial work, and the infrastructure that follows population. Local contractors face the standard Island compliance stack: fall protection, excavation, prime contractor coordination, and the documentation expectations of increasingly formal procurement. We write site-specific plans and subcontractor packages sized for North Island contractors – lean operations that need documentation accepted on first submission, whether the reviewer is a general contractor, the city, or a First Nation development partner.

Tourism & Hospitality

The Salmon Capital of the World backs a genuine visitor economy – fishing charters, wildlife and adventure tours, resorts and marinas, and the restaurants and accommodations serving them. Tourism compliance here has a distinctive signature: public and worker safety interwoven on vessels and in remote settings, marine radio and emergency procedures, seasonal hiring surges, and young workers in first jobs. We build tourism and hospitality programs that seasonal operators can deliver – orientation systems that survive a May hiring wave and procedures that work an hour from the nearest dock.

Healthcare, Retail & Services

As the North Island's service centre, Campbell River's healthcare, retail, and service sectors employ a large share of the workforce – and carry the standard obligations smaller-city employers often underestimate: violence prevention and safe handling in care settings, young worker orientation in retail, and the written program and committee requirements that arrive as businesses grow past 20 workers. We build right-sized programs for service employers – fully compliant, proportionate, and maintainable without dedicated safety staff.

WorkSafeBC Compliance

What Campbell River Employers Need to Know

Campbell River employers work under the same province-wide OHS Regulation, with WorkSafeBC's Island prevention teams administering a region whose industries they know well. The local enforcement picture follows the economy: aquaculture and marine operations face the over-water and vessel-related requirements that come with their hazard profile; forestry and wood processing draw attention to high-severity categories; construction inspections track the city's building activity. Across sectors, the recurring gaps are the familiar ones: undocumented orientations, missing written programs, and emergency procedures that exist in the owner's head but not on paper.

Two North Island realities shape our programs here. First, remoteness: much of the region's work happens well away from immediate emergency response – aquaculture sites, forestry operations, tourism activities – and the Regulation's first aid and emergency transport requirements scale with that distance. Programs written for urban response times fail in North Island conditions. Second, crew turnover: the region's industries cycle workers seasonally and contract by contract, making systematic orientation the single most important – and most commonly cited – compliance element. We build both realities into every Campbell River program we write.
