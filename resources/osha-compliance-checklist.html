Pillar Guide

What's Inside the Checklist

Most businesses fail inspections on the same handful of items — not because they don't care, but because nobody gave them a complete map of what inspectors look for. This checklist is that map, organized into the six program areas where citations concentrate.

1. Written Programs & Documentation

Every required written program — hazard communication, emergency action, fire prevention, PPE assessment — must exist, be current, and match what actually happens on your floor. The single most common failure: a program that describes a facility you don't have.

2. Training & Records

Inspectors don't just ask whether training happened — they ask for proof. The checklist covers the records you need: who, what, when, and how competency was verified. Missing orientation records for new workers is one of the fastest ways to fail an audit.

3. Hazard Controls

Machine guarding, lockout/tagout procedures, fall protection, and chemical storage — verified against your actual equipment, not a generic list. Our risk assessment service goes deeper on this area.

4. Inspection & Maintenance Systems

Recurring inspections must be scheduled, documented, and — critically — show evidence that findings were corrected. A log full of unchecked issues is worse than no log at all.

5. Incident Reporting & Investigation

From OSHA 300 logs to near-miss reporting culture, this section covers the documentation trail regulators expect after any incident — and the systems that prevent the next one.

6. Emergency Preparedness

Evacuation routes, drills, first aid coverage, and emergency contacts — the items that only get tested when it's too late to fix them.

How to Use It

Block 90 minutes, walk your facility with the checklist, and mark every item as compliant, partial, or missing. Anything less than fully compliant goes on your corrective action list with an owner and a date. Repeat quarterly. If you find more gaps than you can close internally, that's exactly what our compliance consulting engagement is for.
