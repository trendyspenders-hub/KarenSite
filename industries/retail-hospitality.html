Retail & Hospitality Safety Plans

Safety programs for stores, restaurants, hotels, and venues — protecting staff who serve the public every day.

Get a Retail Safety Assessment Request a Quote
Know the Hazards
Quick answer: Retail and hospitality employers must document programs for slips and trips, kitchen and equipment safety (burns, knives, fryers), workplace violence prevention — especially for late-night operations — safe lifting, and chemical handling. With young and part-time workers dominating the sector, orientation and training documentation carries extra weight: new and young workers are statistically the most likely to be hurt in their first month.

Risks in Retail & Hospitality

Slips, Trips & Falls

Wet entries, spills, and cluttered stockrooms — the most common claim in the sector.

Kitchen & Equipment

Knives, slicers, fryers, and ovens — burns and cuts send hospitality workers to ER daily.

Workplace Violence

Robberies, aggressive customers, and late-night shifts — prevention programs are legally required.

Lifting & Stock Work

Kegs, boxes, and stockroom shelving — manual handling injuries across every format.

Cleaning Chemicals

Degreasers, sanitizers, and dishwasher chemicals with WHMIS/HazCom training duties.

Young & New Workers

First-job workers with highest injury risk — orientation documentation is scrutinized after incidents.

The Rulebook

Regulatory Requirements

Your program needs to address the standards inspectors actually cite in this sector:

  • Workplace violence prevention program requirements
  • WorkSafeBC young & new worker orientation rules (BC)
  • WHMIS 2015 / OSHA HazCom chemical safety
  • Health-code food safety (complementary to worker safety)
  • Liquor-service & late-night operation requirements

How We Help

  • Multi-location programs One program with location-specific annexes — consistent standards across every store or venue.
  • Fast onboarding systems Orientation checklists that document a new hire's first shift — built for high turnover.
  • Violence prevention Risk assessments, closing procedures, and incident response for late-night and public-facing operations.
Case Study Retail & Hospitality

A three-location restaurant group had a 19-year-old burned on her second shift…

She'd never been shown the fryer procedure — and there was no orientation record to prove otherwise. The claim and WorkSafeBC review that followed cost far more than prevention would have. We built their orientation system, kitchen procedures with photos, and a training record for every position.

Result: Every new hire now has documented first-shift training, and the group's WCB experience rating improved at the next assessment.
