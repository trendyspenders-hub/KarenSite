Transportation & Logistics Safety Plans

Fleet and terminal safety programs for carriers, couriers, and logistics operators — built for the road, the dock, and the audit.

Get a Fleet Safety Assessment Request a Quote
Know the Hazards
Quick answer: Transportation companies need documented programs covering driver qualification and hours-of-service, pre-trip inspection procedures, cargo securement, and incident reporting — under the National Safety Code in Canada or FMCSA/DOT regulations in the US. Generic office templates fail because they ignore road-specific hazards like fatigue, securement, and roadside exposure.

Risks in Transportation & Logistics

Driver Fatigue & Hours

Long-haul schedules, tight delivery windows, and hours-of-service limits that must be documented daily.

Cargo Securement

Shifting loads, improper tie-downs, and overweight axles — a top roadside inspection failure.

Roadside Exposure

Drivers working at the shoulder during breakdowns, inspections, and load checks in live traffic.

Loading Dock Injuries

Dock plates, forklifts, and trailer creep — where warehouse hazards meet the transportation world.

Musculoskeletal Strain

Repetitive lifting, tarping, and cranking dollies — the quiet driver of WSIB/WCB claims in this sector.

Lone & Remote Work

Drivers alone on rural routes need check-in procedures and emergency response protocols.

The Rulebook

Regulatory Requirements

Your program needs to address the standards inspectors actually cite in this sector:

  • National Safety Code (NSC) carrier requirements (Canada)
  • FMCSA / DOT hours-of-service & driver qualification files (US)
  • CVSA roadside inspection readiness
  • Transportation of Dangerous Goods (TDG) / HazMat training
  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation & OSHA general industry standards

How We Help

  • Audit-ready documentation Driver qualification files, inspection logs, and corrective-action records organized for MTO/DOT review.
