Know the Hazards
Quick answer: The most-cited manufacturing safety failures involve lockout/tagout (OSHA 1910.147), machine guarding (1910.212), and hazard communication (1910.1200). Programs fail when written procedures don't match actual machines — the fix is equipment-specific documentation plus verified training.

Risks in Manufacturing

Machine Guarding

Points of operation, nip points, and rotating parts — the source of the most severe amputation injuries.

Lockout/Tagout

Unexpected energization during maintenance kills dozens of workers every year. LOTO failures are heavily cited.

Chemical Exposure

Solvents, coolants, and welding fumes require HazCom programs, SDS management, and exposure controls.

Ergonomic Strain

Repetitive motion and manual material handling drive the most frequent — and costliest — claims.

Noise Exposure

Chronic hearing loss from floor noise requires monitoring and hearing conservation programs.

Powered Equipment

Forklifts and pallet jacks sharing floor space with pedestrians need traffic management plans.

The Rulebook

Regulatory Requirements

Manufacturing programs must address the standards regulators cite most:

  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910 general industry standards
  • 1910.147 Control of Hazardous Energy (LOTO)
  • 1910.1200 Hazard Communication / WHMIS
  • Machine guarding (1910.212) & PPE assessments
  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation Parts 12, 5 & 7 (BC)

Case Study Manufacturing

A food processing plant with 85 employees failed a LOTO inspection…

Their lockout program existed on paper but didn't match a single machine on the floor. We wrote machine-specific procedures for all 23 production lines, retrained maintenance and operators, and set up their annual audit cycle.

Result: Zero LOTO citations in the follow-up inspection and a 40% drop in recordable incidents year-over-year.
