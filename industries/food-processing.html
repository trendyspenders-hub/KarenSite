Food Processing & Service Safety Plans

Safety programs for processors, bakeries, breweries, and commercial kitchens — where production speed meets serious machinery.

Know the Hazards
Quick answer: Food processing safety programs must address machine guarding and lockout/tagout for mixers, slicers, and conveyors; chemical safety for sanitation products under WHMIS/HazCom; and slip prevention for wet production floors. Plants using ammonia refrigeration also fall under process safety requirements. Inspectors from WorkSafeBC or OSHA treat food plants as manufacturing — not kitchens.

Risks in Food Processing & Service

Machine Guarding

Mixers, slicers, grinders, and conveyors — unguarded nip points cause the industry's worst injuries.

Lockout/Tagout

Cleaning and jam-clearing on energized equipment — LOTO failures drive amputation claims.

Wet Floors & Slips

Washdown environments, grease, and ice — slips are the most frequent lost-time injury in food plants.

Sanitation Chemicals

Caustic cleaners and sanitizers demand WHMIS/HazCom training, SDS access, and PPE procedures.

Ammonia Refrigeration

Industrial refrigeration systems carry process-safety and emergency-response obligations.

Burns & Heat

Ovens, fryers, steam lines, and hot CIP systems — plus heat stress in production areas.

The Rulebook

Regulatory Requirements

Your program needs to address the standards inspectors actually cite in this sector:

  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 (Lockout/Tagout) & 1910.212 (Machine Guarding)
  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation Parts 5, 8 & 12 (BC)
  • WHMIS 2015 / OSHA HazCom chemical safety requirements
  • PSM requirements for ammonia refrigeration systems
  • CFIA / FDA food-safety plans (HACCP) — separate but complementary

A bakery received WorkSafeBC orders after a mixer injury…
