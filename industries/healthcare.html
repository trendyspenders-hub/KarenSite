Know the Hazards
Quick answer: Healthcare worker injuries are driven by patient handling, workplace violence, needle sticks, and biological exposure. A complete program requires safe patient handling protocols, violence prevention plans, exposure control plans (OSHA 1910.1030), and training designed for shift-based staffing.

Risks in Healthcare

Needle Sticks & Sharps

Bloodborne pathogen exposure requires engineered controls and exposure control plans.

Patient Handling

Lifting and repositioning injuries are the top source of lost-time claims in care settings.

Workplace Violence

Healthcare workers face violence rates far above other sectors — prevention programs are essential.

Biological Exposure

Airborne and contact pathogens demand infection control protocols beyond clinical standards.

Chemical & Drug Exposure

Disinfectants, sterilants, and hazardous drugs require WHMIS/HazCom programs and safe handling.

Fatigue & Burnout

Shift work and understaffing elevate error and injury risk — scheduling policies matter.

The Rulebook

Regulatory Requirements

Healthcare employers carry specific OHS obligations on top of clinical accreditation:

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens standard (1910.1030)
  • Workplace violence prevention program requirements
  • Safe patient handling & mobility programs
  • Hazardous drug handling (USP <800> alignment)
  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation Parts 5 & 6 (BC)

How We Help

  • Exposure control plans Sharps programs, post-exposure protocols, and annual review documentation.
  • Safe handling programs Equipment assessments, lift policies, and hands-on staff training.
  • Violence prevention Risk assessments, response protocols, and de-escalation training frameworks.
Case Study Healthcare

A care facility with 60 staff had injury claims rising for three straight years…

Patient handling injuries accounted for 70% of claims. We assessed their equipment, wrote a safe handling program matched to resident mobility levels, and trained every care aide in two weeks of staggered sessions.

Result: Handling injury claims dropped 55% in the first year, and the facility passed licensing review with no safety findings.
