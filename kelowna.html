Okanagan

Safety Programs Built for Kelowna's Economy

Kelowna's growth is the defining fact of its economy. The city has been among Canada's fastest-growing municipalities for years, with some neighbourhoods hitting their 2040 population projections two decades early – and residential construction is the engine, driving demand for every trade and reshaping the skyline around the lake. The city serves as the service hub for an Interior Health region of more than 900,000 people. Its tech sector has matured into a genuine cluster fed by UBC Okanagan and Okanagan College's combined 25,000 students. And the Okanagan's original industries keep performing: orchards and field crops across the valley, and a tourism economy built on the lake, the trails, and the mountains.

That combination creates a compliance landscape with real range. A tower crane downtown, an orchard crew at harvest, a fabrication shop, and a hotel pool deck in July all answer to the same WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation – but to very different parts of it. The Okanagan adds environmental hazards the coast rarely faces: extreme summer heat that triggers the Regulation's heat stress requirements, and wildfire smoke seasons that have made air quality exposure a genuine occupational planning issue for outdoor work.

Local Industries

Who We Serve in Kelowna

Construction & Development

Residential construction is Kelowna's biggest economic driver, and the pace has consequences for compliance: crews scaling fast, subcontractors rotating through sites, and prime contractor coordination on projects ranging from infill multiplexes – now encouraged city-wide – to tower developments downtown. Fall protection, excavation, silica, and orientation documentation lead the citation lists, and the region's growth means WorkSafeBC's Okanagan prevention teams are a routine presence on local sites. We write site-specific plans and subcontractor packages for Okanagan builders – sized for lean framing crews and project-scale developers alike, and built to pass prime contractor review on first submission.

Orchards, Farms & Agricultural Operations

The Okanagan's orchards and farms concentrate hazards that surprise operators who think of agriculture as low-risk work: ladders and elevated picking platforms, machinery guarding on tractors and packing equipment, chemical handling under WHMIS 2015, confined spaces in silos and manure systems, and the harvest-period intensity when temporary workers join the crew. Extreme summer heat adds the Regulation's heat stress obligations during exactly the weeks when crews work longest. We write farm programs that treat these hazards with the seriousness they deserve – machinery procedures, chemical management, and harvest orientation systems built for peak-season reality.

Agriculture & Packing

Orchards, vineyards, and the packing houses they feed remain core Okanagan employers, and agricultural work is fully covered by WorkSafeBC: machinery guarding on tractors and harvesters, ladder and elevated platform work in tree fruit, pesticide handling and storage, heat stress during harvest, and the seasonal orientation obligations that come with a workforce that swells every summer. We write farm and packing programs in plain language, designed for seasonal and multilingual crews, with orientation systems that survive a harvest hiring surge.

Hospitality & Tourism

Kelowna's visitor economy – lakefront hotels, the wine tour industry, restaurants, marinas, and four-season outdoor recreation – runs on young workers and seasonal scale. The compliance signature is classic hospitality: kitchen hazards, housekeeping chemicals, late-shift violence prevention, and the orientation obligations attached to every seasonal hire – complicated here by wildfire smoke seasons that add outdoor air quality management to patio, pool, and activity operations. We build hospitality programs that general managers can maintain and seasonal supervisors can deliver.

Technology & Professional Services

Kelowna's tech sector has grown into a real cluster – software, gaming, and digital services firms drawn by talent, lifestyle, and lower costs than the coast. Office employers carry the Regulation's foundational obligations: bullying and harassment policies, violence prevention, ergonomics, emergency procedures, and documented orientation – obligations many tech employers discover only during a claim or a lease audit. We build right-sized programs for office employers: compliant, professional, and finished quickly without drowning a growing firm in paperwork.

WorkSafeBC Compliance

What Kelowna Employers Need to Know

Okanagan employers face the same province-wide OHS Regulation, administered by WorkSafeBC's Interior prevention teams who know the region's industries well. Construction dominates the local inspection picture simply because it dominates the economy – fall protection, prime contractor documentation, and orientation records lead the citation lists. In the agricultural sector, machinery incidents and undocumented seasonal orientations are the recurring themes.

Two Okanagan-specific hazards belong in every local program. First, heat: the valley's summers regularly exceed 35 degrees, and the OHS Regulation's heat stress requirements – assessment, acclimatization, work-rest cycles, hydration – apply to outdoor and hot indoor work alike, from roofing crews to packing line workers. Second, wildfire smoke: recent fire seasons have made smoke exposure a genuine occupational health issue for outdoor workers, and employers are expected to assess and manage it. We build heat and smoke procedures into every Okanagan program – because out here, they are not edge cases.

Harvest season and working at height.
