Safety programs for road builders, utility contractors, and excavation crews — built for the hazards that define heavy civil work.

Know the Hazards
Quick answer: The biggest civil construction safety risks are excavation and trenching collapses, underground utility strikes, struck-by incidents involving mobile equipment, and traffic exposure in live work zones. A compliant civil safety program must include excavation procedures, utility locate documentation, traffic control plans, and documented crew orientations.

Risks in Civil & Infrastructure Work

Excavation & Trenching

Cave-ins kill fast — sloping, shoring, and soil classification procedures are non-negotiable.

Underground Utility Strikes

Gas lines, electrical duct banks, and fibre — locate tickets and safe-dig procedures protect your crew.

Mobile Equipment

Excavators, loaders, and dump trucks working around ground crews — blind spots and swing radiuses.

Live Traffic Exposure

Work zones beside moving traffic demand documented traffic control plans and trained TCPs.

Multi-Employer Sites

Municipal and infrastructure projects stack contractors — coordination duties fall on the prime.

Weather & Remote Sites

Water accumulation in excavations, cold stress, and limited emergency access on rural right-of-ways.

The Rulebook

Regulatory Requirements

Civil and infrastructure work sits under some of the most enforced parts of the regulation. Your program needs to address the standards inspectors actually cite:

  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation Part 20 — Excavation & Trenching
  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation Part 20 — excavation & underground utilities (BC)
  • Traffic control plans & TCP certification requirements
  • BC 1 Call / utility locate documentation procedures
  • COR / Certificate of Recognition programs
