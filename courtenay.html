Safety Programs Built for Courtenay & the Comox Valley's Economy

The Comox Valley — Courtenay, Comox, Cumberland, and the surrounding regional district — supports an economy with unusual breadth for its size. Construction leads the visible growth: the Valley's population keeps climbing, and residential and commercial building has run at pace for years. Tourism employs more than one in ten workers, anchored by Mount Washington's four-season resort operations, the beaches, and a celebrated food and drink scene. The shellfish aquaculture industry gives the Valley one of its genuine global distinctions — the waters of Baynes Sound produce a large share of Canada's farmed shellfish, and aquaculture shows up in the Valley's business patterns at concentrations far above the provincial norm. And over everything sits 19 Wing Comox, the RCAF's West Coast main base, whose presence shapes the local contracting economy.

That breadth matters for compliance because the Valley's employers face almost the full range of WorkSafeBC territory inside one small region. A shellfish farm's over-water work, a cidery's production floor, a resort's lift operations, a military contractor's base access requirements, and a residential builder's fall protection all come from different chapters of the same Regulation — and all get inspected in a region where WorkSafeBC's Island prevention teams know the local industries well.

Alcantara Safety Solutions serves employers across the Comox Valley — Courtenay, Comox, Cumberland, and the rural regional district. We work on-site throughout the Valley, writing programs from scratch against the Regulation and your industry's specific requirements, at a fixed fee quoted before we begin. For a region where most employers run lean teams, our programs are built to be maintained without a safety department.

Local Industries

Who We Serve in Courtenay & the Comox Valley

Construction & Trades

The Valley's building boom shows no signs of slowing — subdivisions expanding around Courtenay, infill in Comox, and commercial growth along the corridor — and local contractors face the full residential compliance stack: fall protection, excavation and servicing work, prime contractor coordination, and the asbestos obligations attached to the Valley's older housing stock. The region's trades are lean operations bidding work where documented safety programs are increasingly table stakes. We write site-specific plans and subcontractor packages that pass prime contractor review on first submission — sized for five-person crews and project-scale builders alike.

Aquaculture & Seafood Processing

Baynes Sound shellfish farming is a signature Comox Valley industry — and a genuinely hazardous one. Aquaculture work means work over cold water: drowning prevention, PFD and immersion suit requirements, rescue procedures, vessel and raft operations, and weather exposure in all seasons. Shore-side processing adds machine guarding, cold storage, sanitation chemicals, and repetitive line work. The sector's federal-provincial regulatory overlap confuses many operators about which safety rules apply — we untangle that, then write programs covering the WorkSafeBC obligations that attach to your crew, your sites, and your vessels' shore-side operations.

Food, Beverage & Agriculture

The Valley's food economy — craft breweries and cideries, dairy and field crop operations, greenhouses and nurseries, bakeries and processors — has grown into one of the Island's most celebrated sectors. Production employers face the standard food-sector stack: machine guarding and lockout, chemical handling, confined spaces in tanks and fermenters (where CO2 is a genuine asphyxiation hazard), and cellar and cold room work. Farm operations add machinery guarding and seasonal worker orientation. We write food and farm programs in plain language, built for producers whose teams are small and whose compliance obligations are not.

Tourism, Recreation & Hospitality

Tourism employs more than 11 percent of the Valley's workforce — Mount Washington's ski and summer operations, the beach communities, adventure operators, and the restaurants and accommodations serving them. Recreation and hospitality compliance has its own signature: public and worker safety interwoven, lift and mechanical systems, seasonal hiring surges, young workers, and emergency response in environments where help takes time to arrive. We build recreation and hospitality programs that seasonal supervisors can deliver — orientation systems that survive a November hiring wave and procedures that work on a storm day.

Defence & Base Support Contractors

19 Wing Comox makes the Valley home to one of the Island's largest single employment sites, and the contractors who maintain, build, and service base facilities work under layered requirements: federal site rules, security clearances, and the OHS obligations that attach to their own workers. Documentation expectations are high and procurement is formal. We prepare the safety programs and pre-qualification packages that let Valley contractors compete for base and institutional work — and keep their WorkSafeBC documentation aligned with their client requirements.

WorkSafeBC Compliance

What Courtenay & the Comox Valley Employers Need to Know

Comox Valley employers face the same OHS Regulation as the rest of the province, administered by WorkSafeBC's Island prevention teams who know the region's industries well. The Valley's enforcement picture follows its sectors: construction inspections track the building boom, food and beverage producers see regular attention on guarding and confined spaces, and aquaculture operations face the over-water and vessel-related requirements that come with their distinctive hazard profile. Across sectors, the recurring gaps are the classics: undocumented orientations, missing written programs, and committee requirements crossed unnoticed as businesses grow.

Two Valley realities shape our programs. First, seasonality: tourism and agriculture both scale their workforces sharply with the seasons, and every seasonal hire triggers orientation obligations from day one — improvised orientation is the single most common compliance failure we see in seasonal operations. Second, lean management: most Valley employers run small teams where the owner is also the safety coordinator, the trainer, and the first aid attendant. Our programs are built for that reality — complete enough to pass inspection, lean enough to actually maintain.
