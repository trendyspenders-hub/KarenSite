Know the Hazards
Quick answer: The biggest construction safety risks are falls from heights, struck-by incidents, electrocution, and caught-in hazards – the four leading causes of serious construction incidents. A compliant construction safety program must include site-specific fall protection plans, subcontractor coordination procedures, and documented worker orientations.

Risks in Construction

Falls from Heights

The leading cause of construction fatalities – roofs, scaffolds, ladders, and unprotected edges.

Struck-By Hazards

Moving equipment, falling materials, and vehicle traffic in congested site conditions.

Electrical & Trenching

Live systems, temporary power, and excavation collapses – the "fatal four" round out here.

Multi-Employer Exposure

Shared sites mean shared liability – your program must cover subcontractor coordination.

Silica & Air Quality

Concrete cutting, demolition dust, and confined-space air hazards with long latency periods.

Workforce Turnover

New workers every project – orientation and training documentation must keep pace.

The Rulebook

Regulatory Requirements

Construction is one of the most heavily regulated sectors. Your program needs to address the standards inspectors actually cite:

  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation – construction operations
  • Fall protection plans & site-specific procedures
  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation Part 11 & 20 (BC)
  • Prime contractor coordination requirements
  • COR / Certificate of Recognition programs
