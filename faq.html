No honest consultant guarantees inspection outcomes – inspectors look at practice, not just paper. What we guarantee is a plan that matches your real operation, plus the training and records systems that make compliance demonstrable. That combination is what actually passes inspections.

A safety manual is a binder of generic policies that may sit on a shelf. A safety plan is a working system: hazard assessments matched to your actual tasks, safe work procedures your crews follow, training records, inspection schedules, and an incident response protocol. Manuals describe safety; plans produce it. Most clients and prime contractors now ask for a plan, not a manual.

Yes. In British Columbia, every employer – regardless of size – must have a health and safety program. Smaller operations qualify for a less formal program, but you're still legally required to have written safe work procedures, training documentation, and a way to report and investigate incidents. Clients and GCs also expect documentation regardless of your headcount.

Yes. We build the documentation foundation COR auditors review: your safety program, safe work procedures, training and inspection records, and incident investigation protocols.
