# Customer Testing Script — Alcantara Safety Solutions Website

**Owner-run usability test.** Prepared 2026-10-21.

You said it yourself: real potential customers testing the site beats any more features. This script gets you there in under an hour, with three to five business owners who are *not* already familiar with the site (ideally people who'd plausibly hire a safety consultant — contractors, warehouse operators, anyone who employs workers in BC).

Two ground rules before you start:
1. **Don't help them.** Resist explaining, pointing, or "well what I meant was…" Their hesitation *is* the data.
2. **Record the session** (phone voice memo is fine) with their permission, or take verbatim notes. "Uh, I guess I'd click…" is gold.

---

## The three tasks (read aloud, one at a time)

**Task 1 — Find a service:** "You run a business with about 15 workers and a WorkSafeBC inspection coming up. Starting from the homepage, find what this company could do for you and how much it would likely cost."

**Task 2 — Understand the scope:** "Pick the service you'd most likely buy. In your own words, what do you actually get, and what happens after you say yes?"

**Task 3 — Request a quote:** "Now ask them for a quote. Go through whatever steps you'd normally take, and stop when you'd expect to hear back from a human."

Then ask three wrap-up questions:
- "On a scale of 0–10, how confident are you that you know what this business would do for you and what it would cost?"
- "Was there any point where you weren't sure what to do next?"
- "What's the one thing that almost stopped you from enquiring?"

---

## What to watch for (don't prompt — just observe and note)

| Signal | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Scrolls up and down the same section | That section isn't answering its question |
| Pauses on "COR," "OHS," or "hazard assessment" | Terminology barrier — the glossary isn't being seen |
| Asks "is this per site or everything?" | The one-site-vs-many explanation isn't landing |
| Hunts for a phone number or email | Contact path isn't visible enough where they're looking |
| Says "I'd just Google it" mid-task | The page didn't establish trust/authority |
| On Task 3, doesn't notice the direct email option | Email fallback isn't prominent enough |
| Quotes a wrong price back to you | Pricing communication is ambiguous |

---

## Scoring (5 minutes after each session)

For each task, mark: **Completed unaided / Completed with hesitation / Abandoned or needed help.**

A task where 2+ of 5 testers hesitate or need help is a real finding. A task only 1 tester struggles with is a watch item.

---

## What to do with the results

Bring me the raw notes (voice memo transcripts, scribbles, anything). Patterns I'll act on immediately:
- Repeated hesitation on the same section → tighten or reposition that copy
- Terminology confusion → expand the glossary or add inline definitions where they looked
- Pricing misquotes → adjust how pricing factors are presented
- Contact-path friction → move/add the email fallback or phone number

What I will **not** do: defend the design. If three people trip on the same step, the step is wrong.
