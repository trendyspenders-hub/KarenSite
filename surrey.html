Safety Programs Built for Surrey's Economy

Surrey is growing into British Columbia's biggest city, and the scale of that growth is hard to overstate. The city holds roughly 38 percent of the region's available industrial land, set records for housing delivery and construction value in 2025, and is absorbing billions in infrastructure investment – including the Surrey-Langley SkyTrain extension now cutting through the city. Its economic strategy targets advanced manufacturing, clean technology, agri-innovation, and health technology, but its present-day muscle is unmistakable: construction, trucking, and supply chain operations, backed by one of the most entrepreneurial small-business communities in the province.

Growth at this pace creates a specific safety problem: companies scale faster than their documentation. A framing crew of six becomes three crews of twenty inside two years. A trucking company doubles its fleet and inherits owner-operator obligations it has never written down. A manufacturer lands a contract with a national customer and discovers the vendor audit requires a safety program that does not exist yet. In Surrey, the trigger for getting serious about safety documentation is usually growth itself – a bid requirement, a pre-qualification questionnaire, or a WorkSafeBC registration letter that arrives after the first big hire.

We work with Surrey employers at exactly that inflection point. Whether you are a two-year-old trades company bidding municipal work for the first time, a fleet operator formalizing a driver safety program, or a manufacturer preparing for a customer audit, we build the program that matches where your company is going – not just where it was when the last binder was printed.

Local Industries

Who We Serve in Surrey

Residential & ICI Construction

Surrey set records for housing delivery and construction value in 2025, and the Surrey-Langley SkyTrain project has added a civil construction layer on top of an already enormous residential pipeline. For contractors, the compliance stakes rise with scale: fall protection on wood-frame mid-rise builds, excavation and trenching requirements on servicing work, prime contractor coordination on multi-employer sites, and – increasingly – documented safety programs as a condition of bidding City of Surrey, school district, and institutional work. We write the site-specific plans, orientation packages, and pre-qualification documentation that let growing contractors compete for bigger work without getting tripped by their own paperwork.

Trucking & Logistics

Surrey is one of Western Canada's great trucking cities, and fleet operations carry a compliance profile that catches many owners off guard: driver orientation and supervision documentation, load securement procedures, yard traffic management, maintenance bay hazards, and the employer obligations that attach to owner-operators working under your authority. Add the hours-of-service and NSC obligations that intersect with provincial OHS duties, and a growing fleet needs more than a handshake and a safety vest. We build driver and yard safety programs scaled to fleets of five trucks or fifty.

Advanced & General Manufacturing

From metal fabrication to the clean technology and advanced manufacturing firms the city is actively recruiting, Surrey's industrial employers face the classic manufacturing compliance stack: machine guarding, lockout, WHMIS 2015, noise exposure, and – for newer operations – the documentation that sophisticated customers and investors increasingly demand before signing. We write manufacturing programs that pass both a WorkSafeBC inspection and a customer vendor audit, because in Surrey's industrial parks, both are matters of when, not if.

Farms & Nurseries

Surrey still holds substantial farmland – nurseries, greenhouses, berry operations, and the equine businesses around them. Agricultural employers are fully covered by WorkSafeBC, a fact that surprises some operators: machinery guarding, confined spaces in silos and manure systems, seasonal worker orientation, and young worker supervision are all regulated, inspectable obligations. We write farm safety programs in plain language, built for operations where the workforce changes with the season.

Healthcare & Community Services

Anchored by Surrey Memorial Hospital and the Innovation Boulevard health-tech corridor, Surrey's health sector employs tens of thousands – and healthcare carries some of the most specific OHS obligations in the Regulation: violence prevention programs, safe patient handling, exposure control for biological hazards, and musculoskeletal injury prevention. We help clinics, care facilities, home support providers, and health-adjacent contractors build programs that address the hazards inspectors actually cite in this sector.

WorkSafeBC Compliance

What Surrey Employers Need to Know

Surrey's rapid growth has a direct WorkSafeBC consequence: the city is full of new employers, and new employers are where registration gaps, missing programs, and undocumented orientations concentrate. Registering with WorkSafeBC is mandatory for nearly every employer the moment they hire their first worker – and the written program requirement kicks in at 20 or more workers for most industries, while high-hazard operations need one regardless of size. Companies scaling through those thresholds mid-growth-spurt often do not notice they have crossed a legal line. We map exactly which obligations apply at your current size and your next stage, so growth never puts you out of compliance.

Two Surrey realities shape our programs here. First, workforce diversity: Surrey's construction and logistics sectors employ one of the most multilingual workforces in Canada, and an orientation that workers cannot understand is an orientation WorkSafeBC treats as not done. We build orientation packages designed for plain-language and translated delivery. Second, procurement pressure: municipal and institutional buyers in the region increasingly weight safety documentation in award decisions, and COR certification is becoming the differentiator on larger bids. For contractors ready to pursue it, we map the full COR path – program build, internal audit, and certifying partner submission.
