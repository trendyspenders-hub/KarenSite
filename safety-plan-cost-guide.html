How Much Does a Safety Plan Cost?

Honest pricing ranges for 2026 — what drives the number, what you should get for it, and how it compares to the cost of getting it wrong.

Get an Instant Estimate Free Consultation
Pricing Guide · 2026

The Short Answer

A professionally written, custom safety plan for a small business typically costs between $1,500 and $4,000. Multi-site or cross-border programs run $4,000 to $10,000+. Ongoing compliance support is usually $500 to $2,000 per month. Downloadable templates sell for $50 to $300 — and are the most expensive option when they fail. The rest of this guide explains what sits behind those numbers.

What Drives the Price

Company size and number of sites

A five-person landscaping crew with one yard is a different project from a 60-person manufacturer with two plants. More workers means more training records, more orientation material, and more inspection systems to design. Each additional site multiplies the documentation.

Industry risk level

Regulators demand more from higher-risk industries. An office safety program is genuinely simpler than an oil & gas or construction program, where fall protection, lockout/tagout, confined space, and equipment-specific procedures all require dedicated written plans.

Jurisdictions you operate in

A plan for a single province or state is the baseline. Operating across Canada and the U.S. means aligning one program to both WorkSafeBC and OSHA — and if you're in a state with its own OSHA-approved state plan (like California or Washington), that layer needs reconciling too.

Timeline

Standard delivery runs two to four weeks. Pre-qualification deadlines and inspection notices create rush work — possible, but priced accordingly. Starting early is the cheapest option every time.

What the Market Charges (2026 Ranges)

These are typical ranges across the North American safety consulting market — useful for budgeting, though every reputable firm quotes fixed-fee per project:

Option Typical Range Best For

Multi-site or cross-border program $4,000 – $10,000+ Growing companies, multiple locations, or Canada–U.S. operations
Ongoing compliance support $500 – $2,000 / month Businesses that want a safety manager on call without the six-figure salary

The Other Side of the Ledger

The meaningful comparison isn't custom plan versus template — it's plan versus no plan. In the U.S., OSHA penalties now exceed $16,500 per serious violation and $165,000 for willful or repeated violations, with amounts adjusted upward for inflation each January. In British Columbia, WorkSafeBC administrative penalties for serious or repeated non-compliance can reach well into six figures. Those are the direct costs; a single lost contract because your safety documentation failed pre-qualification usually dwarfs them.

One serious violation typically costs more than an entire professionally built program. That's the arithmetic that makes this an investment rather than an expense.

What a Quote Should Include

Whatever firm you choose, a proper quote spells out: the written plan itself, site-specific hazard assessments, the forms and record-keeping systems to run the program, orientation and training materials, at least one full revision round, and a guarantee to revise if a client or auditor requests changes. If any of those is missing, the cheaper quote isn't cheaper.
