Janitorial Services Safety Plans

Safety programs for commercial cleaning and janitorial companies — protecting workers across every client site you serve.

Get a Janitorial Safety Assessment Request a Quote
Know the Hazards
Quick answer: Janitorial companies need documented programs for chemical safety under WHMIS 2015 / OSHA HazCom (SDS access, dilution procedures, PPE), slip and fall prevention, lone-worker check-ins for night shifts, and bloodborne pathogen procedures where staff handle sharps or biohazards. Because your workers operate on client premises, your program must also address site-specific orientation at every location you service.

Risks in Janitorial Services

Chemical Exposure

Concentrated cleaners, disinfectants, and floor strippers — dilution errors cause burns and respiratory injuries.

Slips & Falls

Wet floors are your product — signage, footwear, and procedure documentation protect your own crews too.

Lone & Night Work

Cleaners working alone after hours need check-in procedures and emergency protocols.

Biohazards & Sharps

Restrooms, healthcare accounts, and discarded needles — bloodborne pathogen procedures are essential.

Ergonomic Strain

Repetitive mopping, vacuuming, and lifting — MSK injuries dominate janitorial claims histories.

Client-Site Hazards

Every building is different — your program must cover site orientation for each account you service.

The Rulebook

Regulatory Requirements

Your program needs to address the standards inspectors actually cite in this sector:

  • WHMIS 2015 / OSHA Hazard Communication (1910.1200)
  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens standard 1910.1030 (US)
  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation — working alone requirements
  • Client-site orientation & multi-employer coordination
  • Provincial/state janitorial licensing where applicable

How We Help

  • Chemical safety systems SDS libraries, dilution charts, and PPE matrices built around your actual product list.
  • Lone-worker programs Check-in procedures, escalation protocols, and documentation that satisfies working-alone regulations.
  • Multi-site orientation Site-specific orientation templates so every account documents hazards before your crew starts.
