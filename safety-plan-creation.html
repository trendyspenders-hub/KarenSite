Custom Safety Plan Creation

A complete safety program written from scratch for your facility, your hazards, and your regulators.

Request a Quote Call for Advice
Deliverables
Quick answer: A custom safety plan from Alcantara Safety Solutions costs between $2,500 and $7,500 depending on facility size and complexity, takes 4–8 weeks to complete, and includes a full hazard assessment, written safe work procedures, training records, emergency protocols, and rollout support – all written specifically for your facility.

Included in This Service

  • Full hazard assessment of your facility, equipment, and work processes, documented and risk-rated.
  • Written safe work procedures for every high-risk task, in language your crew actually understands.
  • Training matrix & records system so you can prove who was trained on what, and when.
  • Emergency response protocols including evacuation routes, first aid, and incident reporting.
  • PPE program matched to your hazards, with selection and maintenance guidelines.
  • Audit-ready binder (digital + print) organized the way inspectors expect to see it.
  • Health & safety policy signed, dated, and formatted for posting and new-hire packages – as WorkSafeBC expects.
  • Worker orientation package with checklists, sign-off records, and supervisor follow-up steps.
  • Incident investigation procedure with report templates and an immediate-reporting protocol for serious incidents.
  • Annual review framework so your program is re-validated against the OHS Regulation every year.

Every plan covers the nine core elements of a WorkSafeBC health and safety program – management leadership, worker participation, hazard identification, and the rest, all documented and audit-ready.

After You Hire Us: What We Will Need

A short, practical list so work starts fast. None of it needs to be organized — rough versions are fine.

  1. Your existing documents

    Current or outdated safety manuals, site plans, training records, and any correspondence from inspectors or prime contractors.

  2. Site access

    A walk-through of each location the program must cover, with someone who knows the work.

  3. One decision-maker

    A single person empowered to approve scope, review drafts, and sign off on the final program.

  4. The basics

    Crew list with roles, equipment inventory, and any client or bid requirements the program must satisfy.

  5. Timely reviews

    Draft comments within a few days of receipt keep the schedule intact — most delays come from here, not from writing.
